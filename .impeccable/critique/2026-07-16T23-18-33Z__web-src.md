## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment:** AI-slop is largely cleared. The interface no longer reads as a colored wireframe or generic AI dashboard. Patient has an earned functional anchor in `Hari ini`; Caregiver has operational `Terbaru` and `Perlu tindakan` hierarchy; OCR is an evidence workspace; SOS and Faskes communicate truthful boundaries. There is no gradient text, glassmorphism, hero SaaS layout, decorative card grid, neon treatment, random illustration, or decorative motion.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs --json web/src` exited `2` with one warning and zero errors: `overused-font` at `web/src/app/globals.css:701` for Arial. This is a false positive: Arial is scoped to the synthetic `.document-paper` facsimile, while product UI uses Plus Jakarta Sans. The document typography intentionally distinguishes evidence from application chrome.

**Visual evidence:** all 12 final screenshots were inspected at 390×844 and 1440×900. No reliable user-visible detector overlay was available because this session did not expose mutable browser injection. Screenshot, source, Playwright, contrast, and DOM evidence were used instead.

## Overall Impression

The visual-impact pass succeeded because it changed hierarchy and composition, not just color and spacing. The largest remaining opportunity is no longer “make it distinctive”; it is to finish the accessibility and responsive contract around Patient typography/navigation and OCR review controls.

## What's Working

1. **Functional Warmth + Operational Precision is visible.** Patient is warmer, larger, and singularly focused; Caregiver is cooler, denser, and task-led without becoming a generic dashboard.
2. **Trust surfaces feel credible.** OCR remains original-first and pending; chatbot exposes confirmed context; SOS distinguishes active coordination from handled coordination; Faskes shows source and review date without implying live booking.
3. **Orientation and control improved materially.** Active navigation uses visible treatment plus `aria-current`; targets are generally 44/48px; switcher/viewer support Escape and focus return; reduced motion is respected.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Patient safety and support text does not fully meet the locked 18px type contract

**Why it matters:** fatigued, older, or low-vision Patient users still receive some medication, SOS, and chat guidance at 14–17px. The orange `Hari ini` label is approximately 4.33:1 at 15px, just below WCAG AA for normal text.

**Fix:** raise Patient explanatory and safety copy to 18/28, keep smaller type only for genuinely incidental metadata, darken the small orange label or increase its size, and avoid compensating with excessive weight.

**Suggested commands:** `$impeccable typeset`, then `$impeccable colorize`.

### [P2] Patient desktop still uses the mobile bottom dock

**Why it matters:** at 1440px the fixed dock visually crosses the content band and makes desktop feel like a stretched mobile prototype. Focus-scrolled content can also land behind fixed navigation.

**Fix:** make the bottom navigation mobile-only; expose compact Patient navigation in the desktop header or directly below identity. Add scroll padding for fixed mobile navigation.

**Suggested commands:** `$impeccable adapt`, then `$impeccable layout`.

### [P2] OCR mobile comparison and modal semantics are not fully complete

**Why it matters:** tabs reduce overload, but caregivers must still remember evidence across tabs; confirm/reject is far below the first viewport. The `aria-modal` viewer can leak Tab focus to background controls, and the tablist lacks roving focus/arrow-key behavior.

**Fix:** keep decision controls persistently reachable, add lightweight field-to-evidence or reviewed indicators, complete ARIA tab keyboard behavior, and trap/inert modal background focus.

**Suggested commands:** `$impeccable harden`, then `$impeccable adapt`.

### [P2] Mobile Caregiver gives upload precedence over an existing pending review

**Why it matters:** when a document is waiting, `Tambah dokumen` competes with the more urgent decision and teaches the wrong first action.

**Fix:** demote upload to a compact secondary action after the pending task in this state; restore prominence only when there is no pending review.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`.

### [P3] The CSS source retains a superseded override layer

**Why it matters:** the rendered product is cohesive, but duplicate rules and legacy weights make regressions easier and maintenance slower.

**Fix:** consolidate canonical rules, restrict application UI weights to the type contract, and keep document-only serif/Arial declarations inside `.document-paper`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish`.

## Cognitive Load and Emotional Journey

- Patient access/home/SOS is low-load: one dominant action, clear grouping, focused SOS, and a mature reassurance curve.
- Caregiver dashboard/chat/Faskes is low-to-moderate: `Terbaru` and `Perlu tindakan` reduce scanning effort; mobile upload remains one competing intent.
- OCR mobile is moderate-load, improved from high: tabs and persistent pending truth fix chunking, while cross-tab memory remains.
- The strongest emotional improvement is SOS: urgent and serious without dispatch or delivery claims. The strongest trust moment is OCR: original evidence remains the decision reference.

## Persona Red Flags

### Patient mobile user

The primary check-in and SOS choices are clear and reachable, but some safety/routine copy remains smaller than the 18px Patient contract. Do not solve this by turning SOS into a permanent playful red dock.

### Caregiver operator

Patient context and pending work scan quickly. On mobile, upload appears before the pending decision. In OCR, confirmation has no progress/correspondence cue, and the handled SOS band remains visually large on later operational routes.

### Accessibility-dependent user

Visible focus, labels, text-plus-color states, and target sizes are substantially better. Remaining failures are incomplete tab keyboard semantics, modal focus leakage, critical metadata that is too small, and possible focus scrolling behind fixed navigation.

## Minor Observations

- Patient access is the cleanest entry surface.
- Caregiver access CTA falls below the first 844px viewport; this is not blocking but could be tightened.
- Fixed mobile navigation appearing mid-document in full-page screenshots is a Playwright stitching artifact, not a static layout error.
- Tiny facsimile text is evidence content rather than app chrome; the functional zoom is the right accommodation.
- `Koordinasi diambil` correctly avoids implying that the Patient condition is resolved.
- OCR fallback, SOS conflict, and Faskes empty/error/stale states remain difficult to demonstrate through the normal browser fixture path.

## Questions to Consider

1. If Patient desktop is a true desktop surface, why should mobile bottom navigation remain its primary orientation mechanism?
2. Should time, audio/dashboard truth, and SOS limitations ever be among the smallest text on screen?
3. How should a caregiver know each OCR field was actually compared, rather than merely seen before confirmation?
4. When review is pending, should upload remain the first mobile action?

## Recommended Follow-up Commands

1. `$impeccable typeset`: finish Patient 18/28 and critical caregiver metadata.
2. `$impeccable adapt`: replace desktop Patient dock and make OCR decisions persistently reachable.
3. `$impeccable harden`: complete OCR tab and modal keyboard semantics.
4. `$impeccable layout`: correct pending-review precedence on mobile Caregiver.
5. `$impeccable polish`: consolidate CSS after the remaining behavior is accepted.
